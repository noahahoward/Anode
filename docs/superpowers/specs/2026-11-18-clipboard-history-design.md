# Clipboard History (Beta Features)

Written 2026-11-18, on the branch `clipboard-history`. Built, installed and checked on the owner's Mac the same day.

## What it is

Anode remembers what you copy. **Globe (fn) + Space** opens a search panel, drawn like Spotlight's (black at the top fading down into
liquid glass), over whatever you are doing. Type to search, **↑ ↓** to move, **↩** to paste the entry into the app you were in.
It is a Beta Features section, off until asked for.

## What you see and do

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| ↩ | Paste into the app that was in front (⌘V is sent to it) |
| ⌥↩ or ⇧↩ | Paste as plain text |
| ⌘↩ | Put it on the clipboard only |
| ⌘1 … ⌘9 | Paste that row |
| ⌘⌫ | Delete the entry |
| ⌘P | Pin it (pinned entries are listed first and are never dropped by the time or count limits) |
| Tab, ⇧Tab | Next or previous chip (Text, Links, Images, Files, Pinned) |
| esc | Clear the search; a second esc closes the panel |
| a click anywhere else | Closes it |

The panel is a non-activating panel: Anode never becomes the active app, so the app being pasted into stays in front. It fades
in and out. Each row has the source app's icon (an image's own picture, a file's icon), the first line, "detail · app · time ago" under it,
and ⌘1 to ⌘9 at the right. "link", "image", "file" and "text" are searchable words as well as the text itself. Tab and the chips above
the list narrow it to a kind.

The section (Settings → Beta Features → Clipboard History) has: the switch; the status line; the shortcut (changed in Shortcuts…, where
Globe can be recorded); how long entries are kept (30 minutes … 90 days, or forever; a day by default); how many (10 to 5000, 500);
keep images; pause recording; the apps whose copies are never recorded (added to the password managers that always are); Open
Clipboard History; Forget Everything.

## What is never recorded

Privacy comes first, and the rules are in `ClipboardPrivacy` (PowerKit) and applied twice: by the watcher before any data is read, and
by the store itself.

- Copies marked by their app as private: `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType`, `TransientType`, `AutoGeneratedType`, the markers of text
  expanders and 1Password (`nspasteboard.org`), and `com.apple.is-remote-clipboard` (Universal Clipboard, which is fetched only when
  pasted). The markers are never stored with an entry either.
- Copies made in password managers (Passwords, Keychain Access, 1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, LastPass, KeePassXC, Enpass, NordPass,
  Proton Pass, and any app whose bundle id names one), and in apps on the person's own list.
- Anything copied while recording is paused, the screens are asleep or the session is locked: when watching starts again the pasteboard
  is taken as already seen.
- Anything over 10 MB (an entry bigger than that is kept in its essential forms only if those fit), and the whole history is capped at 200 MB.

What is kept is in `~/Library/Application Support/Anode/clipboard-history/`: the folder is 0700, `index.json` and every stored form
0600, and nothing is written until the first copy is recorded. An index that cannot be read starts again empty; a stored form no entry
owns is removed at launch. The log (`clipboard.log`) holds kinds, reasons and bundle ids, never what was copied. The uninstaller removes
the folder with the rest of Anode's data (`--data`).

## How it works

- `PowerKit/ClipboardHistory.swift`: the entry, the rules above, `ClipboardBuilder` (what a copy is: file, link, text, image),
  `ClipboardStore` (the entries, newest first, on disk; the age, count and size limits), `ClipboardSearch`.
- `PowerKit/ClipboardWatcher.swift`: looks at the pasteboard's change counter once a second (with half a second of tolerance); the
  contents are read only after the type strings and the app in front pass the rules. Anode's own write when pasting is marked as its own.
- `AnodeApp/ClipboardHistoryController.swift`: when anything runs (nothing while the setting is off; the timer stops while paused,
  asleep or locked); opening, choosing, deleting, pinning. Everything that touches the Mac is a closure, so it is tested with fakes.
- `AnodeApp/ClipboardPasteboard.swift`: the only code that touches `NSPasteboard.general` and posts a key. ⌘V is sent with ⌘ as the
  only modifier (a Globe key still held does not go with it), to the key that types "v" on the layout in use.
- `AnodeApp/ClipboardPanel.swift`, `ClipboardHistoryFeature.swift`, `ClipboardHistoryRuntime.swift`: the panel, the section, the wiring.
- Shortcut: `Shortcut` gained a Globe bit (`function`). It counts except on the keys that carry the fn flag themselves (arrows,
  function keys, Home, End, Page Up and Down, Forward Delete), so the existing shortcuts are unchanged.

## Measured on this Mac (macOS 27.3)

- macOS lets Anode read the pasteboard without asking (`accessBehavior` is "always allowed" for it); the log says so each time it is turned on.
- ⌘V sent as above pastes into a plain window; a window with no Edit menu ignores it (the test window had to be given one).
- The panel opens centred, 520 wide, its top edge where the system's own ⌘Space panel opens, at window level 101, and the app in front stays in front (checked: it is still the front app while the panel is up).

## The look: measured from macOS 27.3's own ⌘Space panel (2026-11-19)

On this Mac ⌘Space is hosted by Siri ("Search or Ask"), and the panel is two shapes: a bar and, 10 points under it, a card. Both are
liquid glass (the `.clear` style) with a black tint over it; Clipboard History is drawn the same way.

| | measured | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bar | 520 × 87 pt, a full capsule (radius 43.5); text starts 39 pt in, about 21 pt; the picture of the match 33 pt at the right | tint: black 1.0 at the top, 0.61 at the bottom, no rim at the top, a rim at the bottom |
| Card | up to 410 pt, top corners about 38; chips 25 pt tall at 21 pt from its top; rows 62 pt apart; icon 36, title 17, selection a capsule inset 14.5 | tint: 0.55 at the top, 0.30 at 410 pt down; rims at both edges |
| Place | centred; the top edge is where a 137 pt window would be centred at 30% of the screen's height (282 of 1169 on this Mac); the window grows downwards | it can be dragged by the bar and opens where it was left (below) |
| Opening | opacity 0 to 1 evenly over about 0.11 s, while it scales from about 13% larger, about the middle of that 137 pt window, on a spring (period about 0.28 s, damping 0.72, a dip of 0.6% under its size) | sampled from the fitted curve every 1/120 s |
| Closing | opacity 1 to 0 evenly over about 0.11 s while it grows to about 7% larger | |
| Caret | white bar about 2.5 pt wide, as tall as the line, with a glow; blinks at the system rate | |

How the tint was fitted matters. Over the clear glass (which passes a backdrop's brightness B as 0.875 B + 0.125) a black tint of
opacity a leaves (1 - a) of it, and a is read from the system panel over SOLID white, grey and black backdrops down the panel. Fitted to
stripes it came out wrong: the system's glass blurs the card (a hard edge fades over about 20 points; the bar's over 3) and leaks light
into dark areas from far off, so stripes under-read its transparency. The rims are fitted pixel by pixel (two to the point): a hard step in
the last two pixels and a gradual brightening over 4 or 5 points before it.

Dragging (logged from the system's window every 10 ms): it follows the pointer one for one, catches on the horizontal middle and on that
default height within 5 points (a tick from the trackpad), is held until the pointer has pulled it 24 points sideways or 16 up or down,
stops at the screen's edges and under the menu bar (the pointer may go on; it comes back when the pointer does), and opens again where it
was left. It is whole points, so the hard rims stay on the pixels. All of it is `ClipboardPlacement`.

The chips are the kinds in the history (Text, Links, Images, Files, Pinned); Tab moves along them, Shift-Tab back.

## Not done, and why

- Whether releasing Globe after Anode swallows the Space still triggers macOS's own Globe action (the emoji picker, or dictation) cannot
  be tested with posted keys: it needs the real keyboard.
- The system card is frosted: it blurs what is behind it (a hard edge fades over about 20 points) and its bar a little (about 3). The clear
  glass does not blur, so each shape has a backdrop blur under its glass (`BackdropBlurView`: the system's private `CABackdropLayer` with a
  `CAFilter` gaussian blur, found by name and left out if it is not there): sigma 8 for the card and 1.5 for the bar. As the view's own
  backing layer AppKit reset its filters and it blurred nothing; as a sublayer of the view's plain one it works. Measured over the same page
  (white, with small and large type and coloured blocks) against the system's: the colour of a thin block behind the card is within 8%,
  the mean brightness down the card within 0.02, the detail left in flat areas the same. The system's blur has heavier tails than a
  gaussian (see above); not reproduced. NSVisualEffectView materials (a veil of colour, one radius, 33 points) and `.regular` glass were
  tried first and are not used. The card's blur is not one blur: measured with fine stripes (period 20 points) behind it, the contrast
  they keep at each depth above the system card's bottom edge says the blur grows steadily with depth, from sigma about 3 at the rim
  through 4 at 13 points to 6.5 at 61 and beyond (the interior's contrast is 0.038 against 0.30 with no blur). It is made with the
  system's `variableBlur` filter (a `CAFilter`), which reads its radius from the alpha of a mask picture (not its grey); the picture is
  made from the distance to the rounded rectangle's edge and a measured table of the filter's radius against alpha. The blur layer is
  over the glass: under it, the glass's lens at the rim takes what it bends from well inside the card, where the backdrop was already
  blurred, and the rim looked frosted again (a feather of one blur, sharp at the edge and whole 22 points in, had the same fault).
  Over the glass the contrast matches the system's to within 5% from 4 to 67 points. NOT matched: the brightness of the rim zone over
  striped backdrops (the system's is 0.65 in the first 12 points where this one's is 0.45, with the interior 0.49 against 0.45; over
  solid white, grey and black it matches, so it is something that depends on the backdrop's structure), and the stripes' small
  sideways bend at the bottom edge (about 2 points). The selected row is a rounded rectangle (inset 12 points, corners of 24), not a
  capsule: its box and corner profile are the system's to the pixel, and its lift is set to the system's over a dark page.
- The list bends at the card's bottom edge: a deliberate addition (the system's panel has none). A `CIBumpDistortionLinear` on the list's layer,
  20 points deep, pinches the lowest words and icons toward the edge the way the glass's own edge bends what is behind it; the glass is not
  extended past the card's bottom, so that edge shows too. (The filter's scale is 1 for no change: below 1 it magnifies round the line and cuts a
  row's last points off, which is what the first try, at -0.35, looked like; 2 pinches; from 3 up the mapping folds over itself.) The rims are the
  glass's light at its edge, so they are a layer of their own (`ClipboardPanelMetrics.splitTint`) over the list, and the black is under it.
- Spotlight: the system's search (a window of the process named "Siri", at layer 23, alpha 1 while open and alpha 0 but still listed after it
  closes) is closed before this panel opens: Escape posted to that process alone (`postToPid`, so it can reach nothing else), again after 0.12 s
  and 0.3 s if it is still there (the first Escape only clears what was typed). While this panel is open it looks every 0.25 s for the search
  opening over it, which takes no key from it, and goes (`SystemSpotlight`).
- Pasting an entry puts it back on the pasteboard with a form of Anode's own (`ClipboardTypes.ownWrite`), so however the change counter moves
  afterwards, that paste is never recorded as a copy. A copy noticed while Anode itself is the app in front, round the panel (open, or within
  3 s of its going: a closed key panel can hand the key on to a window of Anode's own), is credited to the app that was in front before the
  panel opened, not to Anode (`ClipboardAttribution`). Both came from one row listed with Anode's icon; the cause of that was not found (a
  paste into a plain window recorded nothing new), and `clipboard.log` now says when a copy is noticed with Anode in front and what the app in
  front was at each paste.
- The panel is always the dark glass (as the system's is in a dark appearance); in a light appearance the system's may differ and was not measured.
- Image and file entries are listed and pasted, but image search is by the word "image" only (there is no OCR).
- A copy of Universal Clipboard content is never recorded, so it cannot be searched.
- Each restart of Anode lifts macOS's charge limit for a few seconds and macOS takes about a minute to stop charging after it is set again
  (measured): an update restart can add about 1%. Keeping the limit across a planned restart is not done.
